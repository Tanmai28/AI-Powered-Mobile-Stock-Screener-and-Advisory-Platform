### Stock Screener

A stock screener is a tool that helps you filter thousands of stocks down to a smaller list based on rules you choose—such as price, market value, growth, or technical indicators.

Instead of analyzing every stock manually, screeners quickly surface candidates that match your strategy.

### Why Stock Screeners Are Useful

🔍 Save time by narrowing the market

🎯 Match stocks to your investment or trading style

📊 Combine fundamental + technical criteria

🧠 Remove emotion from idea generation

They don’t tell you what to buy—they help you decide what to analyze next.

### Main Types of Stock Screeners

1. Fundamental Screeners (Company Health) - Used by long-term investors
    Common filters: Market Cap (Large / Mid / Small), P/E Ratio, Revenue or Earnings, Growth, Debt-to-Equity, Dividend Yield

    📌 Example goal: “Find profitable companies growing earnings faster than 10% per year.”

2. Technical Screeners (Price & Momentum) - Used by traders
    Common filters: Moving Averages (50-day above 200-day), RSI (overbought / oversold), Volume spikes, Breakouts / new highs
    
    📌 Example goal: “Find stocks breaking out with strong volume.”

3. Hybrid Screeners - Most popular approach
    Combine: Strong fundamentals, Positive price action
    
    📌 Example goal: “Quality companies that are also trending up.”

### How to Use a Stock Screener (Step-by-Step)
1. Define your strategy
    - Investor or trader?
    - Growth, value, income, or momentum?
2. Choose only a few filters
    - Too many filters = no results
3. Run the screener
    - Aim for 10–50 stocks, not 500
4. Analyze manually
    - Charts, news, financials
5. Make a decision
    - Screeners generate ideas—not buy signals


### 5 common stock screener filters

1. Market Capitalization (Market Cap)
    What it shows: Company size
    Why it matters: Larger companies are usually more stable; smaller ones can grow faster (but are riskier).
        Large-cap: $10B+
        Mid-cap: $2B–$10B
        Small-cap: <$2B

    📌Common use: Avoid very small or very risky stocks.

2. Trading Volume
    What it shows: How many shares trade daily
    Why it matters: Higher volume = easier to buy/sell without price swings.

    📌 Common use: 
            Minimum volume: 500K–1M shares/day

3. Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E)
    What it shows: Price compared to company profits
    Why it matters: Helps identify expensive vs cheap stocks.

    Low P/E → potentially undervalued

    High P/E → high growth expectations

    📌 Common use: Value investing screens.

4. Relative Strength Index (RSI)
    What it shows: Momentum (0–100 scale)
    Why it matters: Identifies overbought or oversold stocks.

    Below 30 → oversold

    Above 70 → overbought

    📌 Common use: Entry timing for traders.

5. Moving Averages (Trend)
    What it shows: Direction of the trend
    Why it matters: Helps trade with the trend, not against it.

    📌 Common use:

    Price above 50-day MA = uptrend

    50-day MA above 200-day MA = strong trend
